Why this panel exists
The two rankings are compared within their overlap — the names that are both trending and scored — so each percentile is over the same population. Ranking each side against its own population instead looks reasonable and is not: the trending list is a top-15 slice while the board is the whole universe, and the mismatch tipped eleven of fifteen names into a single label on a live run. FOMO_CROWDED means the crowd ranks a name well above this model; QUIET_ACCUMULATION means the reverse. Neither is an instruction — a crowded name is not automatically a short, and a quiet one is not automatically early.

Why this panel exists
Both axes are the score's own components, not restatements of price. Y is structural quality — log-market-cap depth, times the liquidity-fit haircut, times the Module F supply multiplier — and X is market confirmation, the blended relative strength against BTC that the score reads. A name high on both is backed by structure and by the tape; high on X alone is momentum without the depth to hold a position; high on Y alone is a quality asset the market has not confirmed. The dividing lines are the board's own medians, so this is a statement about tonight's universe rather than against fixed cut-offs.
CORRELATION & SYSTEMIC RISK —
Correlation pending — ledger/market_intel.json was not produced.
Why this panel exists
Pearson correlation of daily log returns across the top names by conviction, over the nights actually recorded. The number that matters is effective breadth: N² divided by the sum of the correlation matrix, which returns N for an uncorrelated book and 1 for a perfectly correlated one. A fifteen-name book whose members move together is not fifteen positions, and every per-position risk statistic computed as though it were is wrong in the same direction. Pairs with too few overlapping nights are excluded from the mean rather than imputed into it.

Why this panel exists
Market cap is price times units outstanding, so dividing out the price-implied cap leaves the implied unit ratio and nothing else. The arithmetic is exact. Reading it as issuance is an inference: it holds only if the vendor's historical market cap is contemporaneous circulating units at the same published price, with no supply revision or reclassification — which is undocumented and unchecked, because no free issuance feed exists for these tokens. So this is published as a tokenization impulse and never as a mint or a redemption. /rwas/{id}/market_chart answers HTTP 401 below the Basic plan, so this series has no history anywhere except ledger/rwa_flow.csv: a night that was not recorded is gone, and no key bought later recovers it. That is why the column reads accumulating rather than being backfilled.

OFF-HOURS TAPE 24/7 against a shut cash market

Why this panel exists
Between Friday's close and Monday's open, the tokenized tape is the only place price discovery happened anywhere. What is published here is that move, how long the window has been open, how many wrappers agree on its direction and how far apart they are. What is not published is an implied cash-open gap or a confidence in one: converting a tokenized drift into an expected gap needs the underlying's own equity prints as the other half of the pair, and CoinGecko does not carry them. The inputs are recorded so the study becomes possible the night an equity feed is connected.

One line per position, or JSON. Quantity is required; cost and date are optional. btc 0.5 @ 41000 · solana 20 @ 2025-11-04 · {"ethereum":3}
how a line is read
A name resolves against the scored universe first (so BTC works) and against CoinGecko ids second (so bitcoin and lido-dao work). @ 41000 is a unit cost. @ 2025-11-04 is a purchase date, and the cost is then fetched from /coins/{id}/history for that date — one request per dated line, made once and cached. A line with neither has no cost basis, so it shows a weight and a day move and NO open P/L, rather than an open P/L measured from a number nobody supplied.
