## 1.0 — The ledger is a conviction-truncated sample (not on the brief's list; read this first)

Not one of the seven items, and larger than most of them.

`nightly.py:4137` sorts the board by conviction descending. `nightly.py:4166` then
persists `rows[:50]`. So **the ledger is the top 50 names by conviction**, out of the
~235 scored.

Four comments in the same file say otherwise:

- `nightly.py:1486` — "the top 50 by market cap of the 250 fetched — rows[:50]"
- `nightly.py:1689` — "the nightly persists rows[:50] by market cap, so the control is those ~50"
- `nightly.py:1693` — "names churn in and out of the top 50"
- `nightly.py:1779` — "the universe is re-cut at the top 50 nightly"

The data settles it. On 2026-09-14 the recorded rows run from BTC ($1.56 T) down to
龙虾 ($148 M), and include PONS ($392 M), STONK ($168 M) and BR ($151 M) while excluding
PEPE and hundreds of larger names. That set is not a market-cap cut.

**Why it matters.** Tonight's full-universe conviction distribution:

| percentile | p0 | p10 | p25 | p50 | p75 | p90 | p100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| conviction | 4 | 8 | 10 | **14** | 22 | 43 | 100 |

The recorded 50 span 24–100; the 51st name scores 24 and the median of the universe is
14. **The ledger floor sits at roughly the 79th percentile of the board.** Every
statistic computed from `signals.csv` — the IC, the quintile spread, the attribution,
the correlation panel, `market_breadth.json` — is computed on the top ~21% of the
universe *by the very variable whose predictive power is being measured*.

That is textbook range restriction, and it has three consequences:

1. **|IC| is attenuated toward zero.** The measured −0.0505 is the IC *within the top
   conviction quintile*, not across the board. The sign is trustworthy; the magnitude
   is a lower bound on |IC| and the "legs needed" table (111 for 0.03, 250 for 0.02) is
   calibrated against a restricted number.
2. **The quintile spread is mislabelled.** `k = max(3, 50//5) = 10`, so "top quintile
   minus bottom quintile" is the top 10 of the top 50 (≈ top 4% of the board) minus
   ranks 41–50 of the top 50 (≈ the 79th–83rd percentile band). It is not a universe
   long/short, and −98.7 bp should not be read as one.
3. **It breaks Phase 2 before it starts.** §2.2 of the brief asks the backfill to use a
   point-in-time top-N *by market cap*. If the live ledger is a conviction cut, the
   reconciliation test in §2.5 (rank correlation ≥ 0.95 against the 40 live nights)
   would be comparing two different universes and would fail for a reason that has
   nothing to do with reproducing the spec.

**Proposed fix (no hash change — persistence, not scoring).**

- Correct the four comments in the same commit that establishes the fact.
- Record the market-cap rank and the conviction rank on every row so the truncation is
  self-describing, and publish `universe_scored` (235) beside `universe_persisted` (50)
  wherever the panel currently says "universe".
- Raise the persisted set. The honest fix for range restriction is not to re-weight, it
  is to record the cross-section: writing all ~235 rows costs about 4.5× the CSV
  (≈ 2.8 MB/night uncompressed at current column count, ≈ 1 GB/year — too much), so the
  workable version is **a stratified persist**: the top 50 by conviction (unchanged, so
  the existing series stays comparable) *plus* a market-cap-ranked sample of the
  remainder, flagged with the reason each row was kept. The IC can then be reported both
  ways — on the legacy top-50 for continuity and on the full stratified cross-section
  with sampling weights — and the difference between them measures the restriction
  directly.
- Until that lands, every IC surface needs one line: *"computed over the 50
  highest-conviction names, not the full board — a restricted range attenuates |IC|."*

**Decide before Phase 2**, because the backfill's universe rule has to match whatever
this becomes.

## 1.1 — Score saturation at the cap

### What reproduces

The mechanism is real, and it is not a clip. `total = max(0, min(100, int(round(100 *
depth * cm * risk))))` — but `depth ≤ 1`, `a_frac ≤ 1`, `emission_mult ≤ 1` and
`perp_mult ≤ 1.15`, so the clamp is almost never the binding constraint. What saturates
is **CONFIRM**:

```
cm = 0.10 + 0.90 · (tanh(rs_blend / 25) + 1) / 2
```

| rs_blend | 0 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | **65** | 100 | 350 | 1350 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| conviction (depth=1, risk=1) | 55 | 85 | 93 | 96 | 98 | 99 | **100** | 100 | 100 | 100 |

Conviction reaches 100 at **rs_blend = 64.85** and stays there for every value above it.
The last point of the scale spans `rs_blend ∈ [50.97, ∞)`.

Measured against the ledger:

- **347 of 2,240 rows (15.5%)** sit at or above the saturation threshold.
- **43 of 45 nights** carry two or more saturated names at once.
- Max recorded `rs_blend` is **4,621**, against a saturation threshold of 64.85 — a 71×
  range compressed onto one scale point.
- Top-10 rank collisions from integer rounding: **58 across 45 nights**, ~1.3 a night;
  mean distinct values in the top 10 is **8.71 of 10**.

CONFIRM is not floored at 1.0, there is no per-asset override, and there is no cached
value. Saturation is arithmetic, and `tanh(x)` reaching 1.0 in float64 at |x| ≳ 19 does
the rest.

### What does not reproduce

**ZEC and HBAR both at 100 does not appear anywhere in this ledger.** No recorded night
has two assets at 100; 22 nights have exactly one (ZEC, every night since 2026-08-23).

HBAR scored from tonight's live payload:

| reading | brief | reproduced |
|---|---|---|
| RS vs BTC | −9.7 | **−9.69** |
| CONFIRM / "quality" | 0.35 | **0.3838** |
| turnover | 1.8% | **1.76%** |
| FDV/MC | 1.14 | **1.141** |
| conviction | **100 / STRONG** | **14 / AVOID** |

Every inspector reading in the brief matches to the digit. The score does not. HBAR's
`depth` is 0.8831 — just under the 0.90 blue-chip floor — so `a_frac` drops to its 0.40
floor and `100 × 0.8831 × 0.3838 × 0.40 × 0.9965 = 13.5 → 14`. The inspector chain was
correct; the 100 beside it belonged to something else (ZEC is the only name at 100 on
this board). `factorBreakdown()` already guards this case — `index.html:3485` prints
"does not reconstruct: board says N" whenever its own chain disagrees with the published
score — so a genuine board/inspector mismatch would have been labelled on screen.

I am not treating the HBAR line as a defect. The saturation it was reported as evidence
for is a defect, and is quantified above on its own.

### Proposed fix

Replace the tanh with a **cross-sectional rank-percentile** CONFIRM:

```
cm = 0.10 + 0.90 · pctile(rs_blend within tonight's universe)
```

Monotone, bounded, never saturating, and — unlike a logistic squash — it has no scale
parameter to tune, so it cannot be quietly re-fitted. The cost is that the score becomes
relative to the night's cross-section rather than absolute, which must be stated on the
Method page: *a conviction of 90 means "in the top decile of relative strength tonight",
not "this much relative strength".*

Tested against the ledger (same 40 legs):

| | legs | mean IC | t | 95% CI | distinct values in top 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| published (tanh) | 40 | **−0.0505** | −1.87 | [−0.1034, +0.0024] | 8.71 / 10 |
| rank-percentile | 40 | **−0.0260** | −1.04 | [−0.0749, +0.0228] | **10.00 / 10** |

Two honest readings of that, and both belong in the write-up:

1. The ordering at the top is fully recovered — 10 distinct values in every top 10.
2. **This is not evidence.** It is an in-sample re-specification scored on the same 40
   legs that motivated it, and the +0.0245 move is about one standard error (SE 0.025).
   It is also worth noticing that the change moved the IC *toward zero*, not toward
   positive: the compression at the top was pushing extreme-momentum names to the head
   of the board, and those are the names that reverted. De-saturating removes some of a
   negative tilt. It does not create a positive one.

**Do not promote on the strength of that table.** The brief's own sequencing is right:
ship it as a parallel column behind a new hash, run Phase 3 across both specs, decide
after.

**One more result worth having before anyone builds on this.** Re-running the IC with
the *unrounded* product `100·depth·cm·a_frac·em·perp` instead of the published integer
gives **−0.0498 vs −0.0505** — a move of 0.0007, one fortieth of a standard error.
Integer rounding is not costing anything measurable. The saturation fix is a fix to the
*instrument*, and it is worth doing for that reason alone. It is not a fix for the edge.

## 1.2 — Action labels overstate measured edge

### What reproduces

Everything. `signal(c)` at `index.html:2134` maps conviction → STRONG / BUY / HOLD /
WATCH / AVOID unconditionally, and the header's "BUY+ (≥70)" counts `conv >= 70`. The
tier vocabulary is a pure function of the score and consults no measurement at all.

Meanwhile `_compute_edge()` returns `measurable: false` with
`ci = [−0.1034, +0.0024]` and `verdict: "No measurable relationship between conviction
and next-day return."` The panel says it. The board's labels do not.

The site is already ahead of most of this — the Index card carries "arithmetic, not
evidence", `_ATTRIB_BASIS` says it, the Selection Edge panel leads with the interval.
The board's action vocabulary is the one surface that does not.

### Proposed fix (presentation only — no hash change)

Gate the vocabulary on the measurement that already exists:

```
labelsAreActionable = edge.measurable && edge.ci[0] > 0
```

- **Gate open** → STRONG / BUY / HOLD / WATCH / AVOID exactly as today.
- **Gate closed** → descriptive tier names at the *same cuts and the same colour bands*:
  "top decile", "decile 2", … The colour ramp carries the ordering; only the imperative
  is withdrawn.
- One line in the header, always visible, never a tooltip:
  `labels: descriptive — IC not yet distinguishable from zero (40 legs, 95% CI −0.103 … +0.002)`.
- The gate reads `ledger/index.json`'s edge block, which the browser already loads.

Two details that keep it honest:

- **Gate per horizon.** Once Phase 3 lands, "the relevant horizon" is a real choice. The
  score's CONFIRM leg is a 7–200 d blend and the gate should open on the horizon the
  label is claiming, not on whichever horizon happens to clear first. Until Phase 3, the
  only measured horizon is 1 d and the gate should say so.
- **One-sided.** `ci[0] > 0`, not `excludes zero`. An interval entirely below zero means
  the ranking is inverted, which is a finding, not a licence to print STRONG.

Method page note: *"Tier names — what this is: a rank band of the conviction score. What
it is not: a recommendation. The imperative vocabulary (STRONG / BUY+ / AVOID) renders
only while the trailing information coefficient's 95% interval sits entirely above zero
at the stated horizon. It does not today."*

## 1.3 — Clamped / placeholder funding readings

### What reproduces — and what the venue actually publishes

On 2026-09-14, **20 of the 44 rows carrying a funding reading share exactly
10.95% APR**: USELESS, ADA, CAKE, DASH, AR, RUNE, STX, JUP, HYPE, XMR, ETH, XLM, FIL,
PENDLE, RAY, STONK, 龙虾, BTW, AKE, BR.

Two raw rates produce it, and they are the same rate: `0.0001` per 8 h and `0.00005`
per 4 h both annualise to 10.95%, and both are **0.0100% per 8 hours**.

I pulled `https://api.gateio.ws/api/v4/futures/usdt/contracts` live. Of 982 live USDT
perpetuals:

| per-8h-equivalent rate | markets |
|---|---|
| `0.0000` | **383** |
| `0.0001` (0.0100%) | **401** (127 at 8 h + 274 at 4 h) |
| everything else | 198 |

**80% of gate.io's book sits on one of two exact constants.**

**Answer to the brief's question: 0.0100% is gate.io's *default baseline*, not its cap.**
The cap is `funding_rate_limit`, which is `0.02` or `0.01` *per interval* (1–2%) on 734
of the 982 markets — two orders of magnitude away. What 0.0100%/8 h is, is the interest
component of the standard perpetual funding formula
`funding = premium + clamp(interest − premium, ±band)`: when the premium index is at or
near zero, funding rests exactly on the interest rate. `funding_rate_indicative` equals
`funding_rate` on every one of those markets, so the next settlement is predicted at the
baseline too.

The cross-venue spread already recorded in the ledger proves these are not readings of
the same market:

| symbol | gate.io (headline) | another venue, same night |
|---|---|---|
| XMR | 10.95% | Kraken **+94.95%** |
| FIL | 10.95% | Kraken **−55.02%** |
| ADA | 10.95% | Kraken +5.01% |

`apr_spread` is 94.9 for XMR and 66.0 for FIL. A venue at the baseline while another
venue prints ±55–95% is not a market that agrees; it is one venue not quoting.

**Why gate.io is the headline at all**: Binance returned **451** and Bybit **403** on
2026-09-14 (`ledger/venue_health.csv` — both policy-blocked from the GitHub runner), so
`VENUE_PRIORITY`'s third entry supplies 43 of 50 rows. This is a fetch-availability
problem wearing a data-quality costume, and both halves need fixing.

A second clamp is visible in the same data and is not in the brief: **dydx reports
exactly `0.0` APR** for a long list of assets (ADA, XMR, FIL, ZEC …). Zero is as much a
default as 0.0100%, and it currently enters `apr_spread` as a real endpoint — which is
part of why XMR's spread reads 94.9.

### Current scoring impact: zero. Current labelling impact: real.

`classify_regime(10.95)` → `NEUTRAL` (the band is 0 … 12), and `regime_modifier` returns
exactly `1.0` for NEUTRAL. So today a clamped reading and a genuinely inert one produce
the identical multiplier and the score does not move.

That is not a reason to leave it:

1. **The label is a claim.** Module E renders "inside the inert band" — a statement about
   the *market*. The true statement is "the venue did not quote" — a statement about the
   *feed*. The house rule is exactly this distinction.
2. **The margin is 1.05 percentage points.** The NEUTRAL ceiling is 12.0% APR and the
   baseline annualises to 10.95%. A venue moving a market to a 4-hour clock at
   `0.0001` — which 8 markets already carry at 8 h — annualises to **21.9%**, lands in
   ELEVATED, and starts applying a real penalty to a default value. Nothing in the
   pipeline would notice.
3. **Derived statistics are already contaminated.** `apr_trail`, `pos_share`,
   `score_modifier_trail`, `apr_spread`, the carry screen and the funding-regime panel
   all consume the baseline as though it were an observation. ADA's `pos_share` of 0.714
   counts baseline prints as "positive carry nights".

### Proposed fix

Add `funding_suspect` with a stated reason, set when **any** of:

- the per-8h-equivalent rate equals a known venue constant — gate.io `0.0100%` or
  `0.0000%`, dydx `0.0000%` — recorded in a `VENUE_CLAMPS` table with the date each was
  verified against the live endpoint;
- more than `FUNDING_IDENTICAL_N` assets in one night share a rate to full precision
  (tonight: 20; a threshold of 5 is comfortably above coincidence and comfortably below
  tonight's count);
- only one venue reported (`venues_n == 1` — 5 rows tonight: STONK, 龙虾, BTW, AKE, BR).

Suspect readings then:

- are **excluded from the funding multiplier** — routed to the same `1.0` a no-perp asset
  gets, not through `regime_modifier`;
- render in Module E as **"clamped — venue baseline, not a reading"**, distinct from
  "inert", with the venue and the constant named;
- are excluded from `apr_spread`, `apr_trail` and `pos_share`, and the counts of what was
  excluded are published;
- stay in the ledger with the flag. Suppressing them would lose the record of how often
  this happens.

This edits `lavl_perp_mult`'s input path, which is inside the specification capture, so
it **bumps the hash** — while being score-identical on current data. That is worth
stating in the specification history entry: *"behavioural no-op at the time of the
boundary; the boundary exists because the input rule changed, not because a score did."*

**Also fix the cause.** Binance/Bybit are geo-blocked from GitHub's runners. Options, in
order of honesty: (a) accept it and drop gate.io's priority below Kraken and Hyperliquid,
both of which publish a protocol interval and are reachable; (b) note in
`venue_health.csv` that the priority order is *effective*, not nominal, and render the
effective order on the System panel. (a) is a one-line change to `VENUE_PRIORITY` — and
that one line turns out to be invisible to the specification. See §1.8.

## 1.8 — The specification does not capture which venue reaches the score

Found while pricing the §1.3 fix, and it is the same class of hole `nightly.py:228-236`
already documents and closes once ("a specification that stops at a module boundary is
not a specification, it is a description of one file").

`spec()` captures 14 functions and 21 constants. Verified by running it:

```
functions:  score  _lavl_regime  lavl_perp_mult  _tier_for  emission_drag  emission_mult
            funding.{annualize, classify_regime, funding_severity, regime_modifier,
                     rsi, _ramp, _atanh_scale, _num_or_none}
constants:  TIER_CUTS  STABLES  EMISSION_*(4)  funding.REGIME_*(4)  funding.MOD_*(11)
```

Not captured, and every one of them changes published scores:

| not in the spec | what it decides |
|---|---|
| `funding.VENUE_PRIORITY` | **which venue's APR becomes the score's funding input.** Reordering it re-prices 43 of 50 rows tonight. |
| `funding.consolidate()` | the whole headline-selection rule, including the real-interval requirement |
| `funding.INTERVAL_BASIS_REAL`, `funding.VENUE_DEFAULT_INTERVAL` | which venues may supply a headline, and at what assumed clock |
| `nightly._rsi_by_symbol()` | the RSI period and source that gate the squeeze boost inside `regime_modifier` |
| `nightly.perp_context()`, `funding.funding_context()` | how the perps map is assembled before `lavl_perp_mult` reads it |

`regime_modifier`'s *curve* is hashed; the *inputs it is handed* are not. The captured
`funding.rsi` is the pure function, but `_rsi_by_symbol` — which chooses period 7, reads
closes from the ledger, and appends tonight's live price — is outside. Change the period
to 14 and every squeeze boost on the board moves under an unchanged hash.

This is not hypothetical for this audit: §1.3 proposes both a `funding_suspect` rule
(inside the capture, bumps the hash) and a `VENUE_PRIORITY` reorder (outside it, does
not). Shipped together, the boundary would record half the change.

**Proposed fix (no scoring change — instrumentation):** add to `SPEC_FUNDING_FUNCTIONS`
and `SPEC_FUNDING_CONSTANTS`:

```python
SPEC_FUNDING_FUNCTIONS += ("consolidate",)
SPEC_FUNDING_CONSTANTS += ("VENUE_PRIORITY", "INTERVAL_BASIS_REAL",
                           "VENUE_DEFAULT_INTERVAL")
SPEC_FUNCTIONS        += ("perp_context", "_rsi_by_symbol")
```

This **will** move the hash, on an instrumentation change rather than a model change —
precisely the situation `SPEC_EQUIVALENT` exists for. It qualifies for a second
equivalence entry on the same terms as the first: prove it by showing
`spec()["functions"]` is byte-identical for the previously-captured names either side of
the commit, and that no newly-captured value changed in the same commit. Land it
**alone**, before §1.3, so the equivalence is provable rather than tangled with a real
scoring edit.

## 1.4 — Outlier domination in cross-sectional panels

### What reproduces

PONS is real and extreme: `beta_btc = 7.2769`, `rs_blend = 344.26`, `rs30 = 1348.3`,
`funding_apr = −290.175`, turnover 21.38%, market cap $392 M.

### What does not reproduce: the conclusion that it distorts effective breadth

The brief asks for effective breadth to be recomputed after winsorization. It cannot
move, and the reason is in the identity:

```
N_eff = N² / Σᵢⱼ ρᵢⱼ  =  N² / (N + 2·ρ̄·N(N−1)/2)
```

`effective_n` depends on **N and the mean pairwise correlation only**. Beta does not
enter it. RS does not enter it. Winsorizing either changes nothing at all. Today:
`n=18, ρ̄=0.3363 → N_eff = 324/120.9 = 2.68`.

Removing PONS from the set entirely — a far more aggressive intervention than
winsorizing it:

| set | N | ρ̄ | N_eff |
|---|---|---|---|
| all 18 | 18 | 0.3363 | **2.68** |
| drop PONS | 17 | 0.3375 | **2.66** |

It moves *down*. The "one bet" conclusion is not an artefact of one name — it is what
ρ̄ = 0.34 means. As N grows, `N_eff → 1/ρ̄ = 2.97`. **2.68 of 18 is a restatement of the
mean correlation and is robust to every outlier treatment available.**

### What the real defect is

Not the magnitude of PONS's beta — the **observation count behind it**. Betas are
computed over a 30-day window with `CORR_MIN_OBS = 8`, and the ledger does not have 30
nights for every name:

| symbol | n | beta | SE | t | 95% CI | R² |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PONS | **16** | 6.74 | 3.01 | 2.24 | [0.85, **12.64**] | 0.26 |
| LTC | **15** | −0.56 | 0.62 | −0.91 | [−1.77, 0.65] | 0.06 |
| XRP | 30 | 1.81 | 0.24 | 7.65 | [1.34, 2.27] | 0.68 |
| ZEC | 30 | 1.83 | 0.47 | 3.91 | [0.91, 2.74] | 0.35 |
| RAIN | 30 | 0.12 | 0.31 | 0.38 | [−0.49, 0.72] | 0.005 |
| UNI | 30 | −0.08 | 0.45 | −0.17 | [−0.95, 0.79] | 0.001 |

(Recomputed from the ledger; the small n-difference against `market_intel.json` is one
extra close from tonight's row.)

PONS's 7.28 has a 95% interval running from 0.85 to 12.64. It is not an outlier to be
trimmed — it is an estimate with almost no information in it, sitting on a panel beside
XRP's 1.81, which has eight times the precision, rendered in the same weight of type.
RAIN and UNI are worse in a quieter way: R² of 0.005 and 0.001 mean their betas are
noise with a number attached, and they have the full 30 observations.

**Winsorizing at ±3 MAD would replace a wide interval with a narrow-looking point
estimate. That is the opposite of the house standard.** It would make an under-observed
number *less* visibly under-observed.

### Proposed fix (presentation only — no hash change)

1. **Publish the interval, not just the point.** Every beta gets its SE, its n and its
   R² in the tooltip, and the cell is rendered muted (or with the interval inline) when
   `n < window` or `R² < 0.05`. PONS reads "7.3 ±3.0 (n=16)" rather than "7.28".
2. **Winsorize only where a single value would blow up a *shared* axis** — the Factor
   Quadrant and LAVL scatter plots, where one point at x=344 compresses everything else
   into a smear. Clamp the plotted position at the 1st/99th percentile, draw clamped
   points as an arrowhead at the axis edge, and label the axis "clipped at p99". Never
   clamp the *value*; only its position on a canvas.
3. **Leave the correlation panel's inputs alone.** Winsorizing returns before computing ρ
   would change ρ̄ and therefore `N_eff`, which means the published breadth figure would
   silently become a statistic about winsorized returns. If that is ever wanted it is a
   separate, labelled column.
4. **State the finding on the panel**, because it is the useful part: *"Effective breadth
   is N²/Σρ. It is a function of the mean pairwise correlation (0.34) and nothing else —
   removing the most extreme name moves it from 2.68 to 2.66."*

**Should winsorization enter `score()`?** No, and the reason is specific rather than
conservative: the only cross-sectional input to `score()` is `rs_blend`, and §1.1 already
proposes replacing its transform with a **rank percentile**, which is winsorization taken
to its limit — every extreme value already collapses to "the top of the cross-section".
Adding a MAD clip in front of a rank transform would do nothing except add a constant
nobody can justify. Recorded here so it is a decision and not an omission.

## 1.5 — Qualification confusion

### What reproduces

All four surfaces, and they are measuring different things under overlapping names.

| gate | where | rule | tonight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BUY+** | header `#rb-str`, `index.html:2778` | `conviction ≥ 70` | 4 of 235 |
| **Qualified** | board `#skew`, `index.html:2754` | conjunctive gate A∧B∧C | 2 of 235 |
| **Skew Qualified** | System panel `#sys-skew`, `index.html:2755` | **identical to Qualified — same variable, different label** | 2 of 235 |
| **Actionable** | quadrant, `index.html:2938` | `gated OR conv ≥ 70` | 6 of 235 |
| **Index eligibility** | `build_basket()` / Index card | top 10 by conviction, **no gate at all** | 10 |

The conjunctive gate (`_conjunctive_gate`, `nightly.py:825`; `build()`, `index.html:2192`):

- **A** — turnover between **30% and 60%**
- **B** — FDV/MC ≤ 2.0, and proxy-ERA ≤ 1.5 where FDV exists
- **C** — LAVL band not COMPRESS or LIQ TRAP, and turnover < 90%

### Why every top-10 name reads "not qualified"

Gate A demands 30–60% turnover. Conviction rewards `depth` — and deep assets do not turn
over at 30%. Tonight's top 10 by conviction:

| | ZEC | HYPE | UNI | WBT | XMR | PONS | ETH | SOL | XRP | XLM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| conviction | 100 | 86 | 83 | 80 | 67 | 66 | 65 | 61 | 60 | 56 |
| turnover | 7.13% | 4.60% | 11.89% | 1.35% | 1.47% | 29.50% | 5.30% | 5.70% | 4.79% | 5.10% |
| clears gate A | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ (by 0.5 pp) | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ |

**The intersection of BUY+ and Qualified is empty — 0 of 235 names tonight.** The two
qualified names are OP (conviction 22) and 牛来 (conviction 37); both are AVOID.

This is not a bug. It is two near-orthogonal screens sharing one word: conviction
selects for **depth and relative strength**, the gate selects for **high turnover**. A
name can essentially never be both, and the board currently presents that as though it
were a failure of the top 10 to measure up.

### Proposed fix (presentation only — no hash change)

1. **Collapse to two visible states on the board**, as the brief asks:
   - **Tier** — the conviction band (subject to §1.2's label gate).
   - **Liquidity regime** — the conjunctive gate, renamed to what it tests. "Qualified"
     and "Skew Qualified" both become **"active-turnover band"**, since that is gate A
     and gate A is what binds. Rendered as a badge, not as a pass/fail on the row.
2. **Delete the second name.** `#skew` and `#sys-skew` print the same variable; one of
   them should read the other's label or be removed.
3. **Method page table** — the five-row table above, verbatim, with one line each:
   *"BUY+ — what it is: a conviction band. What it is not: a statement that the name
   cleared any liquidity or dilution test."* / *"Active-turnover band — what it is: a
   liquidity-regime filter. What it is not: a quality or conviction screen; on most
   nights it has an empty intersection with the top of the board."*
4. **Index card, one sentence**, as the brief asks: *"This book takes the ten highest
   conviction names and applies no qualification gate — deliberately, because the gate
   selects for turnover and the book selects for conviction, and on 2026-08-05 zero names
   cleared gates A and B while the book still held ten."* (That night is already recorded
   at `nightly.py:1488` as the proof.)

### DefiLlama: feasible, free, keyless — and worth pricing honestly

`https://api.llama.fi/emissions` returns **402 Payment Required**; so does
`/emissionsBreakdown` and `/emission/{protocol}`. The unlocks API is behind DefiLlama Pro
now. The **dataset host is not**:

| endpoint | status | payload |
|---|---|---|
| `defillama-datasets.llama.fi/emissionsProtocolsList` | **200** | 372 protocol slugs |
| `defillama-datasets.llama.fi/emissions/{slug}` | **200** | full schedule: `gecko_id`, `unlockUsdChart` (daily USD series), `supplyMetrics` (`maxSupply`, `adjustedSupply`, `tbdAmount`), `documentedData.tokenAllocation`, dated `metadata.events` |
| `api.llama.fi/protocols` | **200** | 8,252 protocols with `slug` **and** `gecko_id` |

The per-protocol payload carries `gecko_id` itself, so the CoinGecko→DefiLlama mapping is
**read from the source, not guessed from names** — which removes the usual failure mode of
this integration entirely.

**Measured coverage, which is the number that matters:**

- 372 emission slugs;
- **225** resolve against `/protocols`, **211** of those carry a `gecko_id`;
- joined to tonight's 235-name universe: **48 names — 20.4%**.

So this lifts Module B from 0.2% to about **20%**, not to 100%. The remaining 147 slugs
would need one request each to read their own `gecko_id` (a one-time, cacheable 372-request
crawl) and would add some of the rest. A universe of small caps and memecoins is mostly
not in DefiLlama's unlock set at all, and no amount of mapping work changes that.

### Proposed fix (record-only — no hash change)

- New `defillama.py`, same shape as `coingecko.py`: a `_report()`-returning fetch layer,
  no key, status/detail on every call.
- Build the slug→`gecko_id` map from `/protocols`, cache it in `ledger/`, and fall back to
  the per-slug `gecko_id` for slugs `/protocols` does not resolve.
- Columns: `dl_unlock_30d_usd`, `dl_unlock_90d_usd`, `dl_supply_unlocked_pct`,
  `dl_next_unlock_date`, `dl_next_unlock_usd`, `dl_slug`, `dl_coverage`.
- **Unmapped names are null, never zero**, and the per-night mapped count is published
  next to the module so 20% coverage cannot be read as 100%. This is the same rule
  `emission_drag` already follows for unpublished FDV, and for the same reason.
- **Not wired into `score()`.** No hash change. Whether a vesting schedule should move a

  a hope of it.
- Module E's existing copy — "Module B waits on a Dune key" — becomes "Module B records
  DefiLlama unlock schedules for the N of M names DefiLlama covers; Dune remains the
  on-chain override."

## 1.7 — Smaller items

### (a) "DEMO KEY" — what feed, and does it limit or truncate?

**The nightly's CoinGecko credential, and it is probed rather than assumed.**
`nightlyFeedState()` (`index.html:6100`) reads `market_intel.json.session`, which today
holds `{plan: "demo", status: "live", detail: "DEMO key accepted at api.coingecko.com"}`.
`open_session()` (`coingecko.py:143`) pings the Demo host and then the Pro host and
reports whichever answered 200 — so the chip is a measurement. The comment above the
function is already explicit that the chip describes the *job's* credential and not the
browser's, which was a previous defect.

Does it limit or truncate? **Both, and both are already handled.**

- Rate: the Demo tier is ~30 calls/min with a monthly call cap. `coingecko.get()` has a
  429 backoff that distinguishes `rate_limited` from `unreachable`, and
  `fetch_markets()` paginates at `per_page=125` with a 3.5 s delay. The 20-minute job
  timeout was raised from 10 specifically to accommodate that spacing.
- Truncation: `per_page` is capped at 250 on this endpoint, which is why the universe is
  250 fetched → ~235 after stable exclusion. That is a hard ceiling, not a rate effect.
  A Pro key would raise it; nothing else would.
- **The real truncation is not CoinGecko's**, it is `rows[:50]` — see §1.0.

No change proposed beyond §1.0.

### (b) CoinGecko sparkline "55 points"

**The payload is fine; the label is wrong.** Live fetch, 250 markets:

| points | markets |
|---|---|
| **168** (hourly × 7 d) | **247** |
| 164 | 1 |
| 116 | 1 |
| 0 | 1 |

The 55 comes from this page's own decimation. `spark()` is called with `w = 54`
(`index.html:2768`, `5242`), and:

```js
if(arr.length > w){ … for(let i=0;i<w;i++) out.push(…); out.push(arr[arr.length-1]); }
```

168 → 54 + 1 = **55**. The `<title>` is then built from `arr.length` *after* decimation
and reads *"Seven days of price from the CoinGecko sparkline, 55 points."* — which states
the drawn path's resolution as though it were the feed's. One-line fix: keep the
pre-decimation count and say both — *"168 hourly points from the CoinGecko sparkline,
drawn at 55 for width."*

**What the 7 d and RS calculations actually consume: not the sparkline.** Both
`rsBlendOf()` (`index.html:2138`) and `score()`'s `_pct()` (`nightly.py:708`) read the
scalar fields `price_change_percentage_{7,14,30,200}d_in_currency`. The sparkline feeds
the 7D column's shape and nothing else. A defect in it could not move a score.

**But a real null→zero defect is in that path, and it is the house rule's own case.**
Both ports read `t["price_change_percentage_200d_in_currency"] || 0` (JS) and
`float(t.get(...) or 0.0)` (Python). In tonight's payload **19 of 250 assets have a null
200 d field** — BTW, USDGO, EURSAFO, **PONS**, SOFID, APXUSD, AI, EDGE, APYUSD, **STONK**,
ANTFUN, SAFO, CASHCAT, 龙虾, PRL, JPYSC, BP, 牛来, ALFW — almost all of them tokens too
young to have a 200-day history.

For those, `rs200 = 0 − btc200 = −btc200`. PONS's recorded `rs200 = −13.7` is **entirely
BTC's 200-day return with a minus sign**; it contains no information about PONS. It then
enters `rs_blend` at weight 0.20. The same substitution hits `rs30` for one asset and
`rs7`/`rs14` for none tonight.

This is exactly the failure `emission_drag` was written to avoid — an unobserved input
scored as though it were a measured zero — and it is inside `score()`, so fixing it
**bumps the hash**. The fix is the one the codebase already uses elsewhere:
**re-weight the blend over the windows that exist** (renormalise the 0.30/0.25/0.25/0.20
weights across non-null legs) and record `rs_windows_n` per row so a 3-window blend is
distinguishable from a 4-window one. Failing that, null the score. Not zero.

Recommend folding this into the §1.1 + §1.3 parallel spec rather than opening a third
boundary.

### (c) Cap-rotation panel — 139 of 760

**Verified correct: the floor and the exclusion list are applied before ranking.**

Both live inside `coingecko.fetch_categories()` (`coingecko.py:327`), which filters as it
builds the dict. `quant.sector_rotation()` (`quant.py:439`) receives only survivors and
sorts *after* that. There is no path by which a rejected category can influence the
ranking. Order in `fetch_categories`: exclusion-substring → `SECTOR_MIN_MCAP` →
`SECTOR_MIN_COINS` → build row. Tonight:
`"categories: 139 sector(s) of 760 returned"`, `SECTOR_MIN_MCAP = 250_000_000.0`.

**One real finding.** `SECTOR_MIN_COINS = 5` is documented as one of "two floors" but
**never fires**: `/coins/categories` does not return `coins_count`, so `n is None` and the
check is skipped for every category. The code comment says so honestly; the constant's
own comment ("a 'sector' of three tokens is three tokens") reads as though it were
active. Either drop the constant, or fetch `coins_count` from the one endpoint that
carries it and apply it. The `$250 M` floor plus the exclusion list are doing all the
work today, and the panel should say that rather than implying two floors.
