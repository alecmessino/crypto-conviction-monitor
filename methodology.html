CONVICTION MONITOR ← Terminal ● LIVE

Methodology & Performance

How the conviction signal is built — organized as the pipeline that actually runs: collect → engineer features → score → construct the portfolio → analyze. Every figure is derived from the same code that powers the terminal; no estimates unless explicitly flagged.
Single source of truth. The conviction function runs in two places — the live terminal (JavaScript) and the nightly index builder (Python) — and a CI parity test fails the build if they ever diverge. The scoring engine is also frozen by a regression test so a future edit cannot silently shift the model.

2 · Feature Engineering

Raw fields are turned into comparable, bounded features. The shift from 24h momentum to multi-timeframe relative strength is the single largest recent improvement — a coin quietly outperforming BTC over 200 days is a structural signal, not daily noise.

Liquidity · turnover = volume / market cap (fraction)

turnover = volume / market_cap (fraction)
a = 10 + (turnover/0.30)·20  for ≤ 0.30  ·  30 − |turnover − 0.45|/0.15·6  for ≤ 0.60  ·  20 − (turnover − 0.60)/0.60·12  for ≤ 1.20  ·  max(2, 8 − (turnover − 1.20)·4) above

Peaks in the 30–60% band; penalizes illiquidity (<10%) and wash-trading risk (>75%).

Relative Strength (RS) vs BTC · the momentum input

Relative strength isolates an asset's move after removing the market (BTC) drift — the part that is actually the asset's story. Computed on four horizons and blended:

RS_tf = asset_%Δ_tf − BTC_%Δ_tf  for tf ∈ {7d, 14d, 30d, 200d}
rs_blend = 0.30·RS7 + 0.25·RS14 + 0.25·RS30 + 0.20·RS200

200d is included (not 90d) because CoinGecko's free tier returns 90d = null — we exclude it rather than fabricate. The 200d window is what surfaces quiet long-horizon outperformers. Future refinement: log returns and per-horizon volatility normalization so the 200d leg can't dominate in high-vol regimes.

Depth · Dilution · Funding · ERA

  • Depth = clamp((log10(mc) − 6)/4, 0, 1) — log-scaled size, rewards real scale without over-weighting mega-caps.
  • Dilution → Module F, the supply-overhang multiplier. FDV / market cap is the multiple by which supply expands if every scheduled token is issued. It is not a forecast of when, which is why the penalty is capped far below the funding one — dilution is a headwind with an unknown date.
    ratio = FDV / market cap
    drag = 0 for ratio ≤ 1.10, else tanh( ln(ratio / 1.10) / scale )  scale set so ratio = 3.0 lands at drag 0.75
    EmissionMult = 1 − 0.10 × drag  ∈ [0.90, 1.00]
    The gate already knew this mattered — it refuses FDV/MC above 2.0 — but a cliff at exactly 2.0 waves 1.99 through unpenalised and excludes 2.01 outright, expressing nothing in between. A binary admission test is not a way of pricing a continuous risk. Log rather than linear because the difference between 1.2× and 2.4× float expansion is the same kind of difference as between 4× and 8×.

    An unpublished FDV returns a neutral 1.00, not a penalty and not a pass. A token whose fully diluted valuation CoinGecko does not publish has an unknown overhang; scoring it as though it were fully circulating would hand the least transparent assets on the board the best supply grade. The terminal renders that case as a dash rather than as x1.000, so "checked and clear" and "never disclosed" stay distinguishable. This is the second thing in the pipeline that reaches the score, and it moved the specification hash from 596d414706be to 2da60f7efd7b.
  • Funding → RiskMultperp ∈ [0.85, 1.15], from the annualised carry consolidated across venues. The multiplier is 1 + available_adjustment × severity × confirmation, continuous in every input:
    • Severity — how far past the inert band (0% to +12% APR, and down to −15%) the carry sits, as a tanh curve scaled so +40% APR lands at half the available markdown and −40% at 85% of the available boost. Nothing steps: 39.9% and 40.1% APR score alike, while 40% and 400% no longer do.
    • Confirmation, hot side — 24h price extension, additive evidence. Funding above the band already establishes that leverage is being paid for; extension establishes the crowd is also sitting on a move with somewhere to fall. Funding alone earns 50% of the markdown; +10% on the day earns all of it.
    • Confirmation, cold side — 7-period RSI, discriminating evidence. Deeply negative funding reads two opposite ways, and RSI is what separates trapped shorts from shorts who are simply right. Below 45 there is no boost, ramping to full at 60. When RSI is unavailable the boost is withheld outright — absent the discriminator the sign of the right adjustment is unknown, not merely its size.
    • The two sides are deliberately asymmetric. Positive funding is the normal state of a perpetual market — longs pay shorts most of the time, which is the entire reason cash-and-carry is a standard trade — so +40% APR is elevated but unremarkable while −40% is rare and far more informative.
    This replaced a rule that compared the raw per-interval rate against a fixed 0.0005 threshold and then stepped. Two defects, neither fixable by choosing a better threshold: the constant silently encoded an 8-hour settlement clock, so pointed at an hourly venue it triggered near 8% APR rather than 55%; and a step scored materially identical assets 15% apart while scoring wildly different ones the same. The step version also handed a 15% boost to any negative print at all — on the 2026-08-15 board that meant seven assets, four of them for funding between −0.2% and −6.9% APR, and INJ at −51% APR with a 7-day RSI of 2. A boost for being in freefall is the inverse of the trade the rule was written to find. The change moved the specification hash from d600984ec00b to e65f7dc59d55 — history either side of that boundary is two datasets.
  • ERA = 24h-stability proxy (5/ag) until a Dune unlock query replaces it with real emission-vs-adoption data.

3 · Conviction Model

Each eligible asset gets one 0–100 score. The exact same function runs in the terminal (JS) and the nightly builder (Python) and is tested for byte-identical output.

Q Structural Quality · depth (log-mcap)   C Market Confirmation · RS sigmoid   D Risk Adjustment · liquidity × LAVL

Q = clamp( (log10(mc) − 6) / 4, 0, 1 ) · 100
C = 0.10 + 0.90 · ( 1 + tanh(rs_blend / 25) ) / 2  (soft — no hard clamp, rankable within a tier)
R = liquidityFit(turnover) × EmissionMult × RiskMultperp  (liquidity floored to 1.0 for depth ≥ 0.90; EmissionMult is 1.00 when FDV is unpublished)
v2 multiplicative: conviction = clamp( 100 · Q · C · R , 0, 100 )
v1 additive (deprecated): conviction = a + b + (depth·20 + clamp(12+rs_blend·0.4,4,20))
signal: STRONG ≥80 · BUY ≥70 · HOLD ≥55 · WATCH ≥40 · AVOID <40

The switch to multiplication was forced by a ceiling collision in v1: the hard-clamped momentum term pinned PUMP, KAITO and HYPE to the same c_momentum=20, erasing any way to rank a thin-float pump below a blue-chip RS leader on RS strength alone. Under v2, a weak Q (small market cap) or a weak R (thin liquidity) multiplies the whole score down instead of being additively offset, so thin names can no longer overrank quality even on a hot 24h. Confirmation is a soft sigmoid over rs_blend — monotonic, no clamp → names inside the BUY tier stay rankable.

Two things reach the score, and only two. The funding regime multiplier (Module E) and the supply-overhang multiplier (Module F). Everything else added alongside them — trend structure, correlation and beta, liquidity shock, trending divergence, sector rotation and the macro anchors — is observational: recorded per night, rendered on the terminal, read by the position sizer, and reaching the conviction number nowhere. That boundary is enforced rather than asserted: the specification hash is derived from the source of the scoring functions themselves, and a test fails if any name from quant.py appears among them.
Conjunctive gate (eligibility). An asset is "qualified" only if it passes Liquidity (turnover 30–60%), Dilution (FDV/MC ≤ 2.0), and Regime (not COMPRESS / LIQ TRAP) simultaneously. The gate filters eligibility; the score ranks among eligible assets.

Factor Attribution

Every score is decomposed into its component contributions — quality (depth), confirmation (RS sigmoid), risk (liquidity-fit × perp leverage), and rs_blend — stored per asset in ledger/signals.json and rendered in the terminal's Asset Detail drawer. This is what turns the model from a black box into research: you can ask not just "why did UNI rank first?" but "which factor has generated alpha over the last 18 months?" — the prerequisite for factor-level backtesting.

The Factor Decomposition panel decomposes the product the model actually computes, and only that. It shows 100 × depth × cm × a_frac × emissionMult × perp with the running product beside each row, so the bars reconstruct the published conviction rather than describing a neighbouring model. Contributions are rendered as logs, not as 0–100 bars, because the model is multiplicative: in a product the factor moving the score most is the one furthest from 1.0, and a linear bar shows a factor at 0.42 and a factor at 0.95 as "42 and 95" when one of them is halving the score and the other trimming it by a twentieth. An earlier version of this panel showed SIZE, VALUE, QUALITY, MOMENTUM and LIQUIDITY, of which VALUE and MOMENTUM reached the score nowhere. Its MOMENTUM was a 24h reading while confirmation is a 7/14/30/200-day RS blend, so on a mean-reverting day the panel and the ranking pointed in opposite directions with nothing on screen to say which had ordered the board. Both are still shown, in a block labelled observational, on the same footing as Regime in the matrix. Where a name is at the ceiling the panel prints the pre-clamp value ("clamped from 115"), because a decomposition that cannot reconstruct a saturated score is not a decomposition.

4 · Portfolio Construction

The index is a paper portfolio of the top-10 eligible assets by conviction, held with score-proportional weights.

Construction

holdings = top 10 by conviction (eligible universe)
w_i = conviction_i / Σ conviction_j  (j = 1..10)  ·  Σ w_i = 1.0

Hysteresis · Execution · Ejection · Macro (risk overlays)

A Rebalance Hysteresis

A holding is ejected only if it drops to rank ≥ 13 or its conviction falls > 5 points below the rank-11 entrant. A #10↔#11 flip does not churn. Calendar rebalance every 7 days, or immediately on structural exit.

B Execution-Adjusted Counterfactual

Raw paper return stays pure; in parallel an execution-adjusted return deducts 25 bps one-way × one-way turnover at each rebalance. Genesis charges 0. Both curves ship in index.json.

exec_adjusted_return = raw_return − (25bps) × one_way_turnover

C Ejection Alpha Delta

At each rebalance: Δeject = mean(Rentrant) − mean(Rejected realized). Positive cumulative Δ = the rotation dropped underperformers. Full both-over-window figure refines automatically once ≥2 rebalances exist (entry prices persisted in basket.json).

D Macro Liquidity Gate — Passive / Display-Only

RISK-OFF if 7-day global cap < −8%, else RISK-ON. Shown as a regime pill and recorded in index.csv — does not alter holdings. Promoting to an active filter is deferred until enough history exists to backtest it.

Sharpe convention (pinned). At ≥30 days: Sharpe = mean(daily basket return)/std(daily basket return) × √365, rf = 0. Max drawdown = largest peak-to-trough of the cumulative curve. Both recomputed nightly into index.json under risk.

5 · Analytics

Conviction becomes a time series the moment the ledger accumulates daily snapshots. From that series we derive market-level signals that are far richer than a static ranking:

Conviction Breadth & Dispersion

  • Breadth — % of the universe scoring ≥70 / ≥80. A rising market with collapsing breadth is dangerous; a falling market with expanding breadth often marks bottoms.
  • Dispersion σ — stdev of conviction across the universe. Low σ → no edge (everything scores alike); high σ → concentrated opportunity.
  • Persistence — assets scoring ≥70 for 30 / 90 consecutive days. Persistent leadership is more meaningful than one-day leadership.

Conviction Change Feed

Largest 10-day conviction increases and collapses — the homepage-worthy, actionable view. A rapidly improving asset is often more valuable than the highest-scoring static one. Populates automatically once ≥11 daily snapshots exist (needs a Δ10d window).

Index Statistics

Nightly ledger/index.csv + index.json track the basket vs the #2 global market cap (horizon-matched at each rebalance), turnover, execution-adjusted return, ejection delta, and macro regime — a full, reconstructable audit trail.

Active Attribution · Carino-linked

ci,t = activei,t × ( ri,t − bt )   so   Σi ci,t = at = pt − bt
kt = [ ln(1+pt) − ln(1+bt) ] / ( pt − bt )   →   1/(1+pt) as pt → bt
K = [ ln(1+P) − ln(1+B) ] / ( P − B )   →   1/(1+P) as P → B
Ci = Σt ci,t × kt / K   ⇒   Σi Ci = P − B exactly

This panel used to claim it reconciled "by construction" and did not. Per-leg active contributions were summed arithmetically while book_total and equal_weight_total are chained geometrically, so the identity held for one leg and degraded with every leg after it. At twenty legs the residual had reached 312.9bp against a gap of −1266.0bp, a quarter of the number the panel said it explained. Carino linking makes the identity exact; the residual is now zero to floating point and is published beside the total so the claim can be checked rather than trusted.

The attribution decomposes the same legs the performance curve chains, handed over rather than rebuilt. The two used to construct their own leg sets from the same dates under different filters. The curve dropped legs losing more than 10% of the book, the attribution did not, and the attribution applied a minimum-names floor the curve did not. Both happened to report twenty legs, which is a coincidence rather than a coupling. An attribution of a different set of days is not an attribution of that curve, and nothing in the output would have said so.

The book's weights and the benchmark's are over different sets, which is why each leg's own arithmetic is carried rather than re-derived: the book is renormalised over the names that stayed priceable, the equal-weight benchmark is every name present on both nights. A leg with no equal-weight reading has no active return to decompose; it is excluded and counted, because the curve chains the book through such a leg while the equal-weight chain skips it.

The label is unchanged and still the point: arithmetic, not evidence. Exactness is a property of the decomposition, not of the ranking. Over this many legs the ordering is dominated by sampling noise, and treating it as a list of names to drop is how a model gets fitted to its own error.

6 · Observational Modules (G–J)

None of the following touches the conviction score. They inform the regime column, the context panels and the position sizer's ceilings — which shape a book after the ranking exists, and are a different decision from the ranking itself. All of them are pure functions over data this pipeline recorded, in quant.py.

G Trend structure · ADX + Choppiness → strategy

Wilder's ADX and the two directional indicators, computed from bars this repository recorded itself. A 14-period ADX needs 29 nights, not 15 — Wilder consumes the period twice, once to establish the DI and once to average the DX into an ADX. That distinction is what decides whether the column is real or is a shorter reading wearing a longer label, and it is the reason the cell shows 9/29 rather than sitting empty. CoinGecko has no daily-granularity OHLC endpoint at all (30-minute at 1 day, 4-hour to 30 days, 4-day beyond), so a "14-day" ADX taken from it would be a 14-bar ADX over 56 hours. Nothing is back-filled.

ADX is joined to the existing Choppiness Index to select a book: TREND / ALPHA BASKET, GRID / RANGE HARVEST, or COMPRESSING. The two indices measure overlapping but different things — CHOP how much of the range was travelled, ADX how persistently one side won — and where they disagree the label is STAND ASIDE rather than whichever was read first. A market that is directional bar to bar but makes no net progress is a whipsaw, and it is the worst state to run either book in.

H Correlation, beta, and effective breadth

Pearson correlation of daily log returns across the top names by conviction, plus beta to BTC. The number the panel exists to produce is effective breadth:

Neff = N² / Σij ρij

which returns N for an uncorrelated book and 1 for a perfectly correlated one. A fifteen-name book whose members move together is not fifteen positions, and every per-position risk statistic computed as though it were is wrong in the same direction. The position sizer reads the cluster list directly: the first name of a cluster keeps its full target and every subsequent member is halved. Pairs with too few overlapping nights are excluded from the mean rather than imputed into it, and a constant price series returns undefined rather than r = 0 — reporting a stale price as uncorrelated would make the stalest name in the book look like its best diversifier.

I Liquidity shock · the asset against its own baseline

The z-score of tonight's turnover against the same asset's trailing mean and standard deviation. A cross-sectional liquidity screen compares assets to each other and therefore flags the same illiquid names every night, which is a list of facts about the universe rather than news. This flags the night a normally liquid name stops trading. Null below seven baseline nights, and null when the trailing series has no dispersion — a constant history gives an infinite z-score and reports every small change as a crisis.

J Trending divergence · search rank against model rank

CoinGecko orders /search/trending by search interest, so position 1 is not "the best coin" — it is the coin the most people who do not own it yet are looking up. The disagreement between that ordering and the conviction ordering is the reading.

The comparison is made within the overlap — the names that are both trending and scored — and this is the whole correctness of it. Ranking each side against its own population looks reasonable and is not: the trending list is a top-15 slice while the board is the whole universe, so their percentiles do not share a scale. On a live 234-name board that method put eleven of fifteen names in a single bucket, and a classifier that puts three quarters of its input in one category is not classifying. Ranking both sides among the overlapping names only puts them on one scale by construction.

FOMO_CROWDED means the crowd ranks a name well above this model; QUIET_ACCUMULATION the reverse. Neither is an instruction. A crowded name is not automatically a short and a quiet one is not automatically early — the label says two rankings disagree and by how much.

Execution · impact, drag, and the exit

impact (bp) = 10,000 × k × σdaily × √( order / ADV )  +  spread
days to exit = ( order / ADV ) / participation rate

The square-root shape is the well-established part. The coefficient k is not: published estimates of the Almgren–Chriss temporary-impact constant cluster around 1 for liquid equities and nothing here has calibrated it for crypto, so it is a plausible scale rather than a measurement and every figure derived from it is labelled an estimate. Where volatility cannot be read the impact term is reported as unknown and only the spread is charged — an impact estimate with no volatility scale would be a number no input supports. Where volume is unknown the line is refused outright: a sizer that assumes infinite liquidity for a token it has no volume for sizes the least liquid names on the board the largest, which is precisely backwards. Lines that cannot be estimated are counted and excluded from the book-level average rather than entered as free.

Days to exit is often the binding constraint. A position costing 30bp to enter and taking nine days to unwind at 20% of volume is not a 30bp position — the exit is where illiquidity is actually paid, and it is paid in market moves rather than in spread.

ATR stop · a rule-based risk threshold

ATR stop = entry − k × ATR14   (k is the reader's input, default 2)
stop-loss exposure per line = sized notional × ( k × ATR14 / entry )
book exposure = Σ over lines with a recorded ATR14
This is not an invalidation level, and is deliberately not called one. Invalidation is a claim about a thesis: the price at which the reason for holding a position has stopped being true. This board does not know the thesis. Conviction is built from depth, relative strength, liquidity fit, supply overhang and funding, and none of those derive a price at which the case is refuted. What is computed is a distance in units of recent range, which is a smaller and different claim; naming it invalidation would borrow authority the arithmetic does not have.

It is stop-loss exposure, not a risk budget. A budget is a constraint enforced before sizing. This is measured after conviction has already set the line, and nothing in the sizer holds a line to a risk limit. It reports what the book would lose if the rule fired everywhere at once; it does not allocate risk, and describing it as a budget would name a control that does not exist.

k is a convention, not a measurement, and carries exactly the status of the impact coefficient beside it: nothing here has calibrated it against outcomes. What the figure buys is not accuracy, it is that the number exists and is arithmetic the reader can see and disagree with.

The reading keeps its provenance. Every stop carries the date its ATR14 was recorded and the bar count behind it. An ATR detached from its observation date is a number with no shelf life, and a stop derived from a reading several nights stale is a different object from one derived from tonight's.

The transition verifies itself. ATR14 is currently null for the whole universe: quant.atr needs ATR_PERIOD + 1 = 15 bars and the deepest recorded series has 14, so every line correctly reports no derivable stop. That is asserted rather than assumed, by an eligibility invariant that flips on by itself: while no row has the required bars, all-null is valid and the gate says so; the night any row reaches the window, at least one eligible row must carry a finite ATR14 or the gate fails. A row that is not eligible may never carry one, an ATR14 with no bar count behind it is refused, and the window is read from quant.atr_min_bars() rather than restated, so raising the period cannot leave the gate testing a window the code no longer uses. Nothing in it names a symbol or a date: a check keyed to one asset passes forever once that asset leaves the universe.

It refuses rather than substitutes. Where ATR14 is null no invalidation price is shown and the 24h range is not used in its place. The impact term degrades gracefully, a slightly wrong volatility scale gives a slightly wrong cost, but a stop does not: one derived from a single day of range is systematically tighter in a quiet week and wider after one gap, and it would render in the same typeface as the real thing. Those lines are counted against the book total rather than entered as zero, on the same principle as the drag average. As of this writing ATR14 is null for the entire universe: it needs fifteen recorded bars and the ledger has fourteen, so every line correctly reports no derivable stop until the next snapshot lands.

Where a margin percentage is supplied the panel reports implied leverage only. It does not say whether the stop or the exchange arrives first, because that ordering is not computable from initial margin: exchanges liquidate at maintenance margin, which is lower, and at a liquidation price this board has never been told. The panel states liquidation ordering unknown without maintenance margin or exchange liquidation price rather than deriving a winner from the one input that does not determine it.

Contracts · the last mile

Coinbase Derivatives contracts are a fixed quantity of the underlying, not one unit of it. The funding parser computed notional as contracts × price, which is the identity for no contract on the exchange. Twelve HEP contracts at $0.0802 is $4,812, and the parser returned $0.96, so the APR, the 8h equivalent, the hourly rate and the regime badge were all wrong by 5,000× at full confidence. Notional is now contracts × multiplier × price and is printed as the second-largest figure in the panel, because it is the check that catches a wrong multiplier.

A perpetual is not a dated future, and the parser only accepts the first. The panel recovers an annualised funding rate, and only a perpetual-style contract has funding: BIP, ETP, SLP, XPP and HEP debit and credit open positions via a funding rate, while BIT, ET, SOL, HED and the rest are dated futures that expire and pay none. The multipliers coincide in two cases, which is precisely the trap: ET and ETP are both 0.1 ETH and only one of them has a rate to recover. The parser used to default to ET, a dated contract, preselected in a funding panel. It now lists perpetuals only, filtered in code rather than by convention, and defaults to ETP. Dated contracts remain available for contract conversion in the sizer, where converting a dollar target into a count is arithmetic over a contract size and does not depend on funding.

Every row is primary-sourced. Contract code, product name, instrument type, funding capability and contract size are read together off the product table in Coinbase Derivatives' CFTC self-certification filing, and the five perpetuals additionally carry Coinbase's own published specification sheet, which states the funding mechanism in terms. Nothing in the file comes from press coverage.

One source could not be read, and that is recorded rather than worked around. coinbase.com and help.coinbase.com return 403 from the egress proxy in the environment this table was assembled in, so the exchange's own product pages were never fetched here. The rows rest on the two primary sources that were machine-verifiable, and scripts/check_contract_specs.py returns 2, not 0, whenever a source cannot be read or a row cannot be seen: an unseen row is the absence of evidence, and reporting it as a pass is the exact error this file exists to prevent. Each row records code, instrument_type, funding_bearing, multiplier, active_as_of and its sources, and the terminal prints that provenance beside every multiplier it uses.

Verified contract mapping snapshot: 2025-11-26. That is the date the product list was true, and it travels with every mapping the board shows. It is not an enumeration of what Coinbase offers today: the exchange has listed contracts since, including on assets this board scores. No verified contract mapping ≠ contract does not exist. AAVE, ENA and HYPE have no row and are reported as unmapped, which is a statement about this repository's evidence and never about the venue. No code or multiplier is inferred: a row is added only when a primary source states product name, contract code, instrument type and contract size together.

An absent symbol therefore reads "no verified contract mapping", never "spot only" or "no listed contract": those would invent a fact about the exchange out of a gap in a local file. A symbol absent from the table has an unknown multiplier, not a multiplier of 1, and note that 1 is a real contract size here, since BTI is 1 BTC and BCH is 1 BCH, which is why the old silent default was dangerous rather than merely wrong. A multiplier the reader types is accepted and computes, and every figure it produces is stamped USER-ENTERED MULTIPLIER · UNVERIFIED, the regime badge included.

Macro anchors & the fiat bridge

BTC dominance, the ETH/BTC dominance ratio, total market cap ex-BTC (the denominator an alt book is actually measured against, rather than the headline total), the board's median funding APR, and stablecoin velocity.

Velocity is 24h stablecoin volume over aggregate float, taken across the redeemable issuers only — algorithmic and yield-bearing tokens are excluded because their cap can expand without a dollar being wired anywhere, and the indicator is meant to say that fiat arrived. The regime is claimed only when velocity and float growth agree: velocity alone cannot distinguish new dollars from the same dollars moving faster, and float growth alone cannot distinguish deployed capital from parked capital. One without the other is MIXED, which is a reading rather than a failure, and no prior night to compare against is UNCONFIRMED rather than RISK-OFF — treating "no history" as "no growth" would print risk-off on the first night of every deployment.

Time rewind

The ledger has held a full board per night since 2026-08-01 and nothing could look at one. The scrubber replays the recorded conviction matrix across every night on disk. Two things it deliberately does not do: it does not rewind the live-price panels, which are built from a fetch that has no history, and a rewound row reads the funding and regime its own night recorded rather than joining tonight's artifacts onto a three-week-old board. Columns that did not exist on the night being shown render as a dash — including the range Z, which the ledger does not record and which would otherwise show 0.00 and read as "exactly mid-range". The whole page is bordered while rewound, because a nineteen-day-old board read as this morning's is the worst failure the control can cause.

7 · Honest Caveats

  • Live vs snapshot. The terminal's top-10 leaders are computed live; the Index comes from the 06:17 UTC nightly snapshot. Minor intraday ordering differences are expected. The Index is the authoritative record.
  • Module B is a proxy until Dune is connected. Without DUNE_UNLOCK_QUERY_ID, ERA uses 24h stability. Real on-chain unlock data replaces it when configured — never estimated.
  • Perp overlay is server-side. RiskMultperp needs a derivatives feed that can't be called cross-origin. The terminal reads the nightly perp_mult so it stays consistent with the index.
  • Free-tier limits. No intraday, order-book or funding history from the free CoinGecko tier. Modules are scoped to real payload fields; anything we can't source is labeled unavailable (90d RS excluded for this reason). RSI is the one item that came off this list — not by finding a feed, but because the nightly ledger has accumulated enough daily closes to compute one. It is therefore null for any symbol with fewer than eight recorded nights, and the short-squeeze boost cannot fire for those.
  • Funding is scored; positioning is not. Open interest, the OI/price divergence quadrant, the long/short account ratio, the cross-venue spread and the choppiness index are recorded beside every board and read by no scoring function. Adopting any of them would be a separate decision that moves the specification hash. Funding is the exception and always has been — it has reached the score through RiskMultperp since before these columns existed.
  • Carry is not conviction. The cash-and-carry screener ranks the same assets the conviction model penalises, and that is not a contradiction: a high positive funding rate is a reason to be cautious about a directional long and a reason to want the delta-neutral short-perp leg. The two panels answer different questions and are deliberately not reconciled.
  • The modifier reads tonight's print, not the trend — and the counterfactual is recorded. Funding mean-reverts, so one settlement is a noisy estimate of what a position holding the asset would earn. The trailing mean and the share of recorded nights funding was positive sit beside every board and drive the carry screener, but they are not in the score. perp_mult_trail records what the multiplier would have been reading the trailing carry — same function, same confirming legs, applied to nothing. That turns "trend or print" from an argument into a query over two recorded columns, answerable once enough nights exist; adopting it is then a one-line edit inside lavl_perp_mult, which moves the specification hash and draws its own boundary. As of this writing funding_apr has been written on two nights, which is not a basis for changing a published score.
  • The specification spans two files. lavl_perp_mult is a two-line delegation into funding.py, so the hash captures that module's regime boundaries, severity curve, confirmation weights and envelope as well. It has to: for one commit it did not, and replacing the entire step modifier with a continuous surface produced an identical hash — the mechanism built to notice scoring rewrites reporting no change at all.
  • Supply overhang is scored; supply timing is not. Module F prices the multiple by which float expands if every scheduled token is issued. It does not know the schedule. A token that unlocks 40% of supply next Tuesday and one that unlocks it over four years carry the same FDV/MC and earn the same multiplier, which is why the whole envelope is a 10% haircut rather than something a desk would trade off. Real unlock dates are what DUNE_UNLOCK_QUERY_ID would supply, and until it does this is a magnitude without a date.
  • ADX has 9 of the 29 bars it needs. high_24h and low_24h were appended as ledger columns partway through, so the bar series is shorter than the close series and no symbol can produce a 14-period ADX yet. The column reports its progress rather than a number computed over too few bars, and the strategy label falls back to choppiness alone until the bars exist. Nothing is back-filled to close the gap.
  • Every multi-day flow starts at zero nights. Sector rotation, chain rotation and stablecoin float growth are accumulated in ledger/sectors.csv, dex.csv and macro.csv because none of their sources publishes history. On the first night after deployment all three read "accumulating", which is the truth — a 7d rotation computed from one night is a 24h rotation with a longer label.
  • The impact coefficient is a plausible scale, not a measurement. Square-root impact is well established in shape; the constant in front of it is taken from the equities literature and has not been calibrated against crypto fills by anyone here. Treat entry-cost figures as an ordering over the board rather than as basis points anyone was actually charged.
  • The correlation window is short. Effective breadth is computed over at most 30 recorded nights and currently far fewer, so it is a reading about the recent regime rather than a structural property of the book. Correlations rise in drawdowns, which is exactly when the number matters most and exactly when a short trailing window will have understated it.
  • Trending divergence is a disagreement, not a direction. Both labels describe two rankings failing to agree. Nothing in the recorded history yet says which side is right more often — that is a question the accumulating ledger can eventually answer and this page will not pre-empt.
  • Paper only. A research signal system, not investment advice.
  • v2 multiplicative is now live. conviction = 100·Q·C·R replaces the additive model so a saturated clamp can no longer erase discrimination between thin pumps and blue-chip RS leaders. Log-return / per-timeframe volatility normalization for RS is the remaining deferral.
